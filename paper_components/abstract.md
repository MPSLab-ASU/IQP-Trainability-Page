Quantum Circuit Born Machines (QCBMs) offer a natural approach to generative machine learning by leveraging the Born rule. Recent work demonstrates that QCBMs instantiated with Instantaneous Quantum Polynomial (IQP) circuits can be trained entirely classically via the Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD) loss. However, trainability remains constrained due to exponential concentration and barren plateaus. In this work, we leverage Stein's lemma and Lipschitz concentration bounds to establish a rigorous trainability framework for IQP-QCBMs initialized under Gaussian parameters $$\theta_j \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_\theta, \sigma_\theta^2)$$. We derive an analytical lower bound on gradient variance governed by $$\exp(-2\sigma_\theta^2 \lvert\Gamma(a)\rvert)$$, showing that concentration depends on parameter-observable overlap counts $$\Gamma(a) := \{j : g_j \cdot a = 1\}$$ rather than geometric locality assumptions or circuit depth alone. We also show that scaling parameter initialization variance as $$\sigma_\theta^2 = \mathcal{O}(1 / \max_a \lvert\Gamma(a)\rvert)$$, mean as $$\mu_\theta = \mathcal{O}(1 / \sqrt{\max_a \lvert\Gamma(a)\rvert})$$, and MMD kernel bandwidth as $$\sigma = \Omega(n)$$ mitigates exponential concentration and prevents barren plateaus. Finally, we derive a probabilistic concentration bound on gradient deviations showing the conditions under which barren plateaus are likely to happen.
